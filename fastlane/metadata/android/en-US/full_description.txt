AudioLex trains the understanding of spoken words for the case where hearing and understanding come apart: the sound arrives, but it is no longer reliably decoded as speech. That happens after one-sided hearing loss, and a hearing aid alone does not fix it — it makes things louder, not clearer. The path from sound to word has to be practised again.

Two modes, both aimed at the same thing:

Learning mode — a word is played with its text visible. This builds the link between what arrives and what it means.

Exam mode — the word is played, the card stays covered. You rate yourself. That rating decides when the word comes back: after a minute, after ten, after a day, a week or a month.

72 German words and sentences are included: 68 from free speech synthesis, four spoken by real voices. Your own recordings can be added: speak a word or sentence, write it down, train it. Several speakers can be kept apart and switched on or off individually — a familiar voice is a different exercise from a stranger's.

For harder practice, put a background noise under the speech and set the distance between the two in decibels. Three levels do it in one tap: easy without noise, hard with a clear speech advantage, advanced with the noise above the speech. One noise is included, recorded inside a bus. Your own can be recorded or imported as WAV files.

For headphone training there is a channel choice — left, right or both — which helps when only one ear should be trained. Over a Bluetooth hearing aid, stereo is summed to mono; the app detects this and shows the channel choice as ineffective instead of pretending otherwise.

What AudioLex does not do: it requests no internet permission, has no account, no trackers, no ads. Vocabulary, ratings and session history stay on the device — not as a promise, but because the app has no technical way to send anything. A backup writes your own recordings, your own noises and the session history to a ZIP file in your documents when you ask it to. What happens to that file afterwards is up to you.

AudioLex is a practice tool, not a medical product. It replaces neither an audiologist's advice nor an ENT examination.

The app was built for one person with exactly this problem. It shows: the interface is German, it is plain, and it does not explain itself.
